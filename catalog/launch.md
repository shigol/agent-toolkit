# launch

**Purpose:** Plan product launch marketing, feature announcements and adoption follow-up.

**Invocation:** automatic

**Route:** Plan launch marketing

**Synonyms:** product launch; launch marketing; go-to-market plan; GTM; feature announcement; launch checklist; Product Hunt; waitlist; early access; customer changelog

**Use when:** Preparing a public product or feature announcement, organizing launch channels and phases, checking a marketing-site launch, or translating already shipped changes into customer-facing drafts.

**Do not use when:** Starting a shell process, fixing an application startup error, deploying a backend service, or certifying engineering readiness and rollback procedures without project-specific checks.

**Overlaps and default:** product-strategy owns overall direction; create-prd owns requirements. launch owns marketing timing, distribution and adoption; Humanizer can edit announcement prose. verification-before-completion governs technical completion claims. Upstream related skills and optional partner tools are not installed or authorized by this admission.

**Requirements:** Product context, intended users, scope, timeline and available channels. Read existing product-marketing context and use supplied facts before asking. Core planning needs no external account. Read references/shipped-changes.md for release digests and verify customer availability, deployment and feature flags before making announcement claims. Read references/site-launch-qa.md for marketing-site switches; actual browser, analytics or platform checks need available tools and scoped authorization. Preserve project deployment rules. Publication, outreach, paid services and production changes require the applicable existing authorization; a planning request produces drafts, not a live launch. References require pinned setup beyond a cached entry file.

**Expected output:** A scoped phased marketing plan, channel choices, readiness questions, launch checklist and post-launch adoption actions; announcement work produces grounded drafts and pages-to-update lists.

**Positive example:** "Plan the beta announcement and customer onboarding for our new collaboration feature next month."

**Negative example:** "Fix a Python subprocess that exits immediately when started."
